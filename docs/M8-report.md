## 0. Where the brief and the research differ

Every number in the brief checked out. Three diagrams needed tightening to match the research:

1. **Context adjustment (§11) is two different mechanisms.**
   - Studies 1–3 compare each player with himself, using player × team × season fixed effects plus venue, opponent, calendar and kickoff terms.
   - Study 4 adjusts profiles for opponent, venue and league environment. It **deliberately does not apply own-team strength**, because doing so absorbed form and made profiles less persistent.
   - The page shows both, with the reason.
2. **"Transfer history" (§15) is not a model input.** Model A *learns from* earlier moves. Its inputs are three-season output, age, minutes, both clubs' strength, both leagues and a promoted-club term.
3. **Fixture congestion (§18) is partly visible.** League fixtures are in the data; cup matches aren't. The chip says "Cup matches" and the text explains the partial view.

## 2. Glossary data structure — `content/glossary.json` (single source)

```
categories   [id, label] × 5: metrics · context · statistics · modelling · recruitment
terms{key}   term, full, cat, aka[] (search synonyms), used[] (studies), tools[] (similarity · transfer · live · case),
             method (methodology section), short (popover), plain (Simple), analyst (Technical), why, example
```

**Terms:** 50 in total: 41 carried over from M0 (text unchanged) and 9 added from the findings:
- shots / 90
- headed share
- in-box share
- correlation
- percentile
- similarity score
- league effect
- transfer prediction (Model A)
- context-adjusted output

Each new term's example is a real result from the research. For example: in-box share puts Bruno at the 3rd percentile; correlation is r = 0.018 across 595 players.

**Categories:** the brief's four, plus **Football context** (home advantage, behind closed doors, opponent strength, adjusted output). Those terms are research concepts, not metrics or statistics.

**The build** writes two files:
- `football/js/glossary.js`: the popover data, 15 KB.
- `football/glossary.html`: a static page of 50 entries, so search engines see every definition. It has DefinedTermSet JSON-LD.

It fails on a missing field or an unknown category, study, tool or methodology section.

**Each entry shows:** short definition · Simple · Technical · Why we use it · In the research (example) · Used in (studies, tools and the methodology section, all links) · synonyms.

**Search:** substring match over term, full name, synonyms and definitions, plus a category filter and deep links (`/football/glossary#mae`). Tested:

## 5. Mobile implementation

- **Popovers** become a bottom sheet under 640 px: full width, anchored to the bottom, with a scrim; tapping the scrim closes it. Focus moves to the close button; Escape and the scrim both close it. Tested at 390 px.
- **Glossary page:** search, chips and stacked entries.
- **Methodology:** the four-cell blocks, stats and metric tiles collapse to one or two columns; flow diagrams turn vertical.

## 6. Accessibility

- **Structure:** one `h1` per page; `h2` per category or section, `h3` per entry or block; `<dl>` for definitions and stats.
- **Search:** labelled input with a live result count; category chips are buttons with `aria-pressed`.
- **Popovers:** popover triggers carry `aria-haspopup` / `aria-expanded`, and the popover is a labelled dialog.
- **Flow diagrams:** each carries an `aria-label` reading the whole flow.
- **Contrast:** dashed "what it doesn't solve" cells differ in label text, not colour alone.
- **Tested:** all 15 routes at 1440 / 390 px: 0 console errors, 0 failed requests, 0 horizontal overflow.
