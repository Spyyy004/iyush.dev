I reconstructed what my transfer model would have told Manchester United on 1 June 2024 — using only information that existed that day.

Two signings:

→ Joshua Zirkzee: predicted 0.46 xG + xA per 90. Actual: 0.47.
→ Manuel Ugarte: predicted 0.02. Actual: 0.14. A clear miss.

The miss is the more useful half. The model had a documented weakness (it under-predicts low-output midfielders moving into the Premier League), and Ugarte was signed for defensive work that attacking data can't see. A recruitment model can be useful without being a complete player model.

Zooming out to every candidate who joined a Premier League club that summer: 4 of 4 strikers and 5 of 6 midfielders landed inside the model's 80% range. One retrospective, not proof.

It's the applied end of a five-study project on how much of football performance belongs to the player versus the context — home advantage, opponent strength, similarity across leagues, and what survives a transfer. The finding that surprised me most: statistical similarity is good for finding candidates but added nothing to predicting how they'd do after a move.
