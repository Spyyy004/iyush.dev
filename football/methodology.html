Methodology

How do we know?

One dataset, five studies, and one rule throughout: compare each player with himself, separate signal from noise, and never let a prediction see the future.

DataWhat's in the dataset — and what isn't

Every player in every match of the Premier League, La Liga, Serie A, Bundesliga and Ligue 1 from 2015/16 to 2024/25 — the “frozen” study data. New seasons (2025/26, and 2026/27 as it happens) are added every week but kept separate, so they never change the original results.

Coverage: 18,011 matches (18,008 with usable player data), 160 teams, 8,348 players, 525,328 player-matches, ~452,000 shots; five leagues, 2015/16–2024/25 (frozen). Live seasons 2025/26 (complete: 1,752 matches) and 2026/27 (in progress) are appended weekly and never alter frozen results.

Per player-match: minutes, goals, assists, shots, , , , , , cards, granular position, home/away, opponent, kickoff. Per shot: location, xG, situation, body part, last action, assister.

Sources: Understat (player and shot data); football-data.co.uk (pre-match Pinnacle odds; market-average closing odds for live seasons); fixturedownload.com and openfootball (kickoffs); Wikipedia/Wikidata and OpenStreetMap (stadiums); Open-Meteo ERA5 (weather); dated crowd-restriction calendars per league; and, for Study 5, the CC0 Transfermarkt-derived dataset (dcaribou/transfermarkt-datasets) for dates of birth.

Not in the data: defensive actions (tackles, pressures), passes completed, carries, possession, attendance figures, injuries, fees, contracts. That's why the studies focus on attacking output and exclude goalkeepers.

MethodsWithin-player comparisons, signal vs noise

Compare each player with himself.

Instead of comparing different players, every comparison is one player, at one club, in one season — at home versus away, or against strong versus weak opponents. Differences in ability and team quality cancel out.

Separate signal from noise.

Some players will always look like home specialists or big-game players just by chance — the same way some people flip more heads. Each individual estimate is weighed by how much real information it carries, and the persistence of any “trait” is checked against shuffled data.

Individual effects get calibrated standard errors (pseudo-splits or shuffles within each player's own matches), DerSimonian–Laird between-player spread and ; persistence is measured against shuffled data.

Measure opponent strength without peeking.

Opponent strength comes from betting odds published before kick-off, built up match by match so that no match ever informs its own rating.

A sequential market rating from pre-match odds (each match uses only earlier matches), checked against an xG rating, Elo, opening odds and points per game. Effects are reported per +1 of the rating.

Correct for testing many things at once.

Test thousands of players and some will look special by luck. The bar is raised to account for that, and every headline effect is re-run on fake dates to make sure it isn't an artefact.

Benjamini–Hochberg corrections and permutation tests (); (e.g. closed-doors dates shifted 2–4 years) for every headline effect.

Leakage155,282 checks, 0 changed ratings

A model that secretly uses information from after the event looks brilliant and is useless. Every rating and every prediction here only uses what was known at the time.

Opponent ratings were re-computed after scrambling later results: 0 changed ratings in 155,282 checks. Every Study 5 prediction uses only information available at the decision date.

Understat's match “forecast” turned out to be post-match (r = 0.96 with the match's xG difference). It was never used in any model.

ValidationLocked unseen seasons, scored once

The transfer model was finished before the last three seasons of moves were opened, then tested once on them. Similarity was checked without anyone's opinion: can it recognise the same player after he moves league?

Study 5: the final model was fixed before the (2023/24–2025/26) were opened and scored once on 142 unseen moves: 0.096 vs 0.129 for the (improvement 0.033 xG + xA per 90, CI 0.018–0.049), 0.76 vs 0.58; slightly better on the unseen seasons than on earlier ones. Robust to minutes thresholds, loans, refitting each season and an alternative target (xGChain).

Study 4: — three-season profiles find the same player in the top 9% of a destination league's role pool after a league move; adjusted inputs beat raw ones for players who changed league.

Live tracker: every Monday Studies 2 and 3 are re-fitted on seasons the studies never saw, and their predictions are checked. See the live tracker.

The transfer model's typical error on the unseen seasons was 0.096 xG + xA per 90, against 0.129 for “he'll do what he did”. And every Monday the home and opposition findings are re-tested on new matches — see the live tracker.

PipelineRebuilt every Monday

Every Monday morning the pipeline fetches the weekend's Understat data and odds, reconciles kickoffs, rebuilds the live tables and opponent ratings, re-runs the tracker, refreshes the Study 4 similarity profiles and rebuilds the datasets this site reads.

The site never re-runs the research. It reads precomputed, validated outputs; the similarity search and transfer calculator are browser ports of the study models, checked against the Python originals (transfer model: 60 fixture cases, maximum prediction difference below 0.0001 xG + xA per 90).

LimitsWhat this can't tell you
  • Observational data: effects are associations within carefully matched comparisons.
  • Output only — no defending, pace, possession, injuries, fees or contracts; goalkeepers and (in Study 5) defenders excluded.
  • The period is mostly one season, so per-player crowd estimates are noisy; no attendance figures.
  • Cup matches and fixture congestion are not in the data.

Still open

  • Study 1's final temperature placebo check, hand-written weather conclusion and freeze.
  • Study 4's user-curated “known similar pairs” check.
  • Study 5's percentage-based league effect, to be validated on moves after 2025/26.
CorrectionsMade along the way
  • Understat's win/draw/loss “forecast” was at first described as pre-match; it is post-match (r = 0.96 with the match's xG difference). It was never used in any model.
  • Study 2's “stable part” magnitudes are upper bounds: the cross-season covariance estimator is biased upward even with no true persistence (found in Study 3). Conclusions unchanged.
  • Study 5: a promoted-club term was first read as an imputation artefact; it is a real effect (players joining promoted clubs produce more than the club's rating predicts). The early “centre-forwards drop most” reading was mostly (96% vs stayers, no clear role effect).
